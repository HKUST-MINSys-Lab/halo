# Published performance ceilings for IMU/accelerometer HAR by evaluation regime

Fact table for the related-work section. Compiled 2026-09-22. Every number below was read either from a local PDF under `references/` or from a page/PDF actually fetched during this session; the source column says which. Where a fetch failed or a paper is paywalled, the entry says "not retrieved" rather than guessing. Metric names are given exactly as the paper reports them (accuracy, macro-F1, weighted-F1, F-measure, AUROC differ substantially and are not interchangeable).

Abbreviations: acc = accuracy; mF1 = macro-averaged F1; wF1 = weighted (support-averaged) F1; LOSO = leave-one-subject-out; k-fold = random k-fold over windows (subject-dependent); OW/NOW = overlapping / non-overlapping sliding windows.

## R1 — Closed-set labels, same subjects (random / within-subject split)

This is the regime that produces 95–99.9% numbers. Two mechanisms inflate it: (i) subject identity is shared between train and test, and (ii) overlapping sliding windows are shuffled before the split, so near-duplicate windows straddle train/test (Hammerla & Plötz, UbiComp 2015, DOI 10.1145/2750858.2807551; Tello et al. 2023).

**R1 summary.** The ceiling is not a ceiling; it is a leak. Every ≥99% closed-set number located in this search comes from either pooling all subjects and shuffling (Kwapisz 2011; Gupta 2021; Gholamiangonabadi 2020; Micucci 2017 5-fold), or additionally shuffling *overlapping* windows (Mekruksavanich 2021 OWCV; Huang et al.; Wang et al.; the 2026 PAMAP2 CNN-BiLSTM). Three papers report both protocols on the same model and dataset, which makes the size of the artefact explicit: UCI-HAR 99.39 → 93.94 acc (OW 10-fold → NOW LOSO), MHEALTH 99.85 → 85.1 acc, UniMiB A-9 88.41 → 73.17. The widely-quoted "99% on UCI-HAR" therefore does not come from the dataset's own protocol (which is subject-disjoint and yields ~96–97%), it comes from papers that discarded that protocol. The trap for a related-work section is quoting a 10-fold number as if it were the dataset's difficulty.

**R2 summary.** Placement/device shift is the second-largest known degradation after label shift, and it is larger than subject shift. The two peer-reviewed anchor numbers are HHAR (10-fold 91.0 → LOUO 66.7 → one-device-model 56.4 wF1; Stisen et al. 2015) and REALDISP (>90% ideal → ≤65% self-placement → <50% induced displacement; Banos et al. 2014). BenchHAR 2026 gives the modern SSL-era version of the same story: cross-subject 70 F1 vs cross-location 40–50 F1 on the same pooled corpus. The orientation component alone halves UniMTS's zero-shot mF1 (34.3 → 17.6). The trap: "cross-device" results are often reported with subjects seen in training (RealWorld per-subject models, REALDISP same 17 subjects), which understates the deployment gap; and conversely many "cross-dataset" results (LIMU-BERT-X, DAGHAR) mix placement, device and subject shift and cannot be decomposed.

**R3 summary.** Once subjects are held out, the closed-set ceiling is dataset-specific and much lower than R1 suggests: ~96–97% acc on UCI-HAR (6 coarse locomotion classes, waist phone, a dataset now described as saturated), ~88–89 mF1 on MotionSense, ~79–85 on RealWorld and PAMAP2 (depending on sensor set and window), ~55–61 mF1 on USC-HAD and HHAR (wrist, 2 s windows), 45–69 on Opportunity depending on whether the task is 4-class locomotion or 18-class gestures, and 27–32 mF1 on WISDM-19 phone. Two 2026 benchmarks that standardise the protocol across 14–30 datasets both land at a mean macro-F1 in the 60s (WHAR Arena 67.7; BenchHAR 61.3 cross-dataset). The trap in this regime is metric mixing (weighted-F1 or accuracy on imbalanced sets can sit 10–20 points above macro-F1 on the same predictions) and window length (2 s windows in the Haresamudram protocol produce numbers ~20 points below 10 s-window numbers on the same dataset).

**R4 summary.** For genuinely unseen datasets + subjects + devices with text-matched labels, the published ceiling is UniMTS's 43.5% accuracy / 34.3 macro-F1 averaged over 18 datasets (NeurIPS 2024), with AnyMo (2026, arXiv) roughly on par on a different 14-dataset subset. Everything that reports dramatically higher zero-shot numbers relaxes one of the three "unseen" axes: HARGPT (0.79 F1) uses 2–4 easy classes and only holds out users; ZARA (81 mF1) retrieves labelled windows from other subjects of the same dataset; SensorLM (0.84 AUROC but 0.29 macro-F1) evaluates on the pretraining population with class names in the captions; NormWear reports AUROC only. Class-split ZSL on a single dataset (Ghosh 2026: 0.58 mF1 on 4 unseen PAMAP2 classes) sits between. The trap is the word "unseen": most 2024–2026 papers do not state whether the test label strings (or paraphrases) occur in the pretraining text corpus, and the two that report the highest numbers (SensorLM, ZARA) demonstrably have label leakage by construction. A second trap is accuracy on imbalanced unseen-class sets, which Ghosh 2026 shows can sit 15 points above macro-F1 for the same predictions.

## Specific questions

**1. Where does "99% on UCI-HAR" come from?** Not from the dataset's own protocol. Anguita et al. (ESANN 2013, Table 4) report 96% on the official subject-disjoint test set (21 train / 9 test volunteers, 2947 test windows), and every subject-disjoint deep model since lands at 95.8–97.5% (SDFL Table 3; Sensors 2021 PMC8706653; SensorLLM Table 2 gives 93.2 macro-F1). The ≥99% figures come from papers that pooled all 10,299 windows and ran random k-fold: the most explicit is Mekruksavanich & Jitpattanakul (*Sensors* 2021), whose 4-layer CNN-LSTM scores 99.39% under 10-fold CV on 50%-overlapping windows (Table 8) but 96.70% under LOSO with the same windows (Table 10) and 93.94% under LOSO with non-overlapping windows (Table 11). The *Sensors* 2021 feature-fusion paper (PMC8706653) states in its own text that its 99.56% arises "because the data division method of the database source was not used; rather, data were merged and subjected to a tenfold cross-validation", versus 97.49% on the official split. Tello et al. (2023) catalogue further 97–99% UCI-HAR / WISDM / USC-HAD claims (Huang et al. 97.12 F1 with a 7:1:2 merged split; Wang et al. 98.28 F1) and trace each to shuffled overlapping windows. So: **the 99% number is both subject-leaked and window-leaked**; the honest same-dataset number is ~96–97% accuracy.

**2. Best published cross-subject closed-set macro-F1 per dataset** (strongest verified source; accuracy given where no macro-F1 exists):
- UCI-HAR: 93.2 mF1 (Attend, official split; SensorLLM Table 2); 95.8–97.5% acc range on the official split (SDFL Table 3; PMC8706653).
- WISDM: 0.810 subject-wise F1 (2019 watch, 18 cls; Yuan et al. 2024 Table 3); 83.89% acc LOSO on 2011 phone data (Jordao 2018); 32.2 mF1 best on WISDM-19 phone (WHAR Arena 2026).
- PAMAP2: 85.4 mF1 9-fold LOSO wrist (RelCon 2025 Table 3) / 86.2 mF1 multi-IMU subject-independent (SensorLLM Table 2); 0.789 wrist 8-class (Yuan et al.).
- Opportunity: 69.1 mF1 locomotion 4-fold LOSO (RelCon); 45–48 mF1 gestures LOSO (Bock et al. 2022 Table 1; 2026 Table 2).
- MotionSense: 89.25 mF1 5-fold subject-wise (Haresamudram et al. 2022 Table 3); 87.9 mean over 17 models (WHAR Arena).
- RealWorld: 84.92 mF1 LOSO (DeepConvContext, ISWC '26 Table 2); 0.792 wrist-only (Yuan et al.).
- USC-HAD: 61.2 mF1 subject-independent (SensorLLM Table 2); 57.9 (Haresamudram Table 3); 75.89% acc LOSO (Jordao).

## What a fair comparison for a zero/few-shot enrollment model looks like

Grounded in the above, a fair table has four properties. (1) **Subject-disjoint and window-disjoint by construction**, stated per dataset, with macro-F1 as the primary metric; every closed-set reference number placed next to ours must come from a protocol of the same kind (Haresamudram 2022 / Yuan 2024 / SensorLLM 2025 / WHAR Arena style), never from a 10-fold or challenge-split paper — the UCI-HAR row should read "~96% acc / 93 mF1", not "99%", and the Opportunity row "45–69 mF1", not "0.915". (2) **The three "unseen" axes reported separately** — unseen subjects, unseen device/placement, unseen labels — because the literature shows they cost different amounts (subject ≈ 5–15 points, placement/device ≈ 20–30 points, label-open-set collapses supervised models to 3–8 mF1) and papers that conflate them (ZARA, SensorLM, HARGPT) look far stronger than they are. (3) **The right competitor per regime**: for k=0 with unseen labels the honest ceiling is UniMTS at 34.3 mF1 (18 datasets) or AnyMo at 29.5 (14 datasets), so a text-aligned k=0 result in the 30s–40s is state-of-the-art and anything reported in the 80s must be checked for label-text or same-dataset retrieval leakage; for k≥1 enrollment the honest ceilings are the fully-supervised cross-subject numbers in R3 (e.g., 89 MotionSense, 85 RealWorld/PAMAP2, 61 USC-HAD, ~67 mean over 30 datasets), and the relevant baselines are the same encoders under identical k-shot budgets (Yuan et al. Fig. 3a; UniMTS few-shot Fig. 4; Thukral et al. 2025) rather than their full-shot numbers. (4) **Explicit label-text hygiene**: state whether any test label string or paraphrase occurs in the pretraining text/caption corpus, since none of the 2024–2026 zero-shot papers except the class-split ZSL work controls this, and SensorLM shows AUROC can read 0.84 while macro-F1 is 0.29. Under these rules the fair claim is comparative and regime-specific — "at k=0 we match/exceed the 34 mF1 UniMTS ceiling on unseen datasets; at k shots we close X% of the gap to the 60–90 mF1 subject-disjoint supervised ceiling without touching the closed-set softmax" — and any "99%" appearing in the table is flagged as an R1 artefact.
